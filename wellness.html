STORAGE & SAFETY • ARTICLE 01 BY LICENSED PHARMACISTS 5 Ways to Store Medicines Safely in Nigerian Heat Nigeria’s high temperatures and humidity can affect how medicines are kept at home. While many medicines are designed to remain stable, heat, sunlight and moisture can degrade quality over time. Proper storage helps maintain effectiveness and keeps your family safe. This guide offers general tips for households and does not refer to any specific prescription product. Information provided by our licensed pharmacists for community education. KEY STORAGE PRINCIPLES 1 Store in a cool, dry place below 30°C where possible. Choose the coolest room, away from direct sunlight and away from stoves or generators. Avoid window sills and car dashboards. 2 Keep medicines in their original packaging with the leaflet. The packaging is designed to protect from light and moisture and helps you check expiry and storage instructions clearly. 3 Avoid bathrooms and kitchens for long-term storage. These areas often become hot and humid during cooking or bathing, which may affect packaging and labels over time. 4 Separate medicines for adults and children and keep all medicines out of reach and sight of children. Use high shelves or locked boxes and never transfer medicines to food or drink containers. 5 Check expiry dates monthly and follow label guidance on refrigeration. Only refrigerate if the label states so. Do not freeze unless specifically instructed. Dispose of expired packs safely via our physical pharmacy. If you travel within Nigeria, carry medicines in a shaded bag and avoid leaving them in parked vehicles. When in doubt about storage after heat exposure, visit our licensed physical pharmacy for guidance. Never use medicines that have changed colour, smell, or texture. PREVENTION • ARTICLE 02 BY LICENSED PHARMACISTS Understanding Blood Pressure - Lifestyle Tips for Prevention Blood pressure is the force of blood against artery walls as the heart pumps. Over time, consistently high pressure can strain the heart and blood vessels. Many people may not notice symptoms, which is why regular checks and healthy daily habits are important for prevention. This article explains general concepts and lifestyle measures and does not prescribe treatment. Information provided by our licensed pharmacists. LIFESTYLE HABITS THAT SUPPORT HEALTHY PRESSURE Reduce salt gradually: Taste food before adding salt, limit salty snacks, suya spice with high salt, stock cubes, and processed meats. Choose fresh foods and herbs for flavour where possible. Move daily in ways you enjoy: Aim for brisk walking, dancing, farming activities, or cycling for at least 30 minutes most days. Start slowly and build consistency rather than intensity. Maintain a healthy weight and waist: Combine balanced portions of whole grains, beans, vegetables, fruits, and lean proteins. Avoid sugary drinks and late-night heavy meals. Manage stress and sleep: Practice calm breathing, short breaks, community support, and keep a regular sleep schedule. Limit excessive caffeine late in the day. Avoid tobacco and limit alcohol: Smoking harms blood vessels. If you drink alcohol, keep it minimal and seek support to quit or reduce. Check regularly: Adults should have blood pressure checked at least yearly at a clinic or at our licensed physical pharmacy (where allowed). Keep a simple record of results. Prevention works best as a family and community effort. Encourage regular walks together, cook with less salt, and support each other to attend health screenings. Visit our physical pharmacy for general wellness counselling — our licensed team is available. SEASONAL PROTECTION • ARTICLE 03 BY LICENSED PHARMACISTS Malaria Prevention - Protect Your Family This Season Malaria remains a major health concern in Nigeria, especially during rainy seasons when mosquitoes breed more easily. The illness is caused by parasites spread through the bite of infected mosquitoes. Prevention focuses on reducing bites and breeding sites, while early care helps avoid severe illness. This guide is for community education and does not recommend specific medicines. Information provided by our licensed pharmacists. FAMILY PROTECTION STEPS Sleep under an insecticide-treated net every night, ensuring nets are tucked in and free of holes. Ensure children and pregnant family members are prioritised for net use. Eliminate standing water weekly: empty containers, old tyres, flower pot saucers, and gutters. Cover water storage tanks and repair leaking pipes. Screen windows and doors and close gaps where mosquitoes enter. Use fans where possible and wear long sleeves and trousers in the evening. Keep surroundings clean: clear bush near homes, manage waste, and support community sanitation. Consider indoor residual spraying through approved community programs. Seek care early for fever: If any family member develops fever, chills, headache, or body pain, visit our licensed physical pharmacy or health facility promptly for testing. Avoid self-medication. Support community awareness: Share prevention messages with neighbours, schools, and religious groups. Encourage antenatal care for pregnant women for tailored prevention advice. Community action multiplies protection. When households combine net use, clean environments, and early testing, mosquito numbers and malaria cases often fall. Keep a simple calendar to track net checks and weekly clean-up days. WHEN TO SEE A DOCTOR Fever, chills, or severe headache lasting more than a day Vomiting, inability to eat, or high fever in children under 5 Symptoms during pregnancy — seek care urgently Any positive rapid test needs clinical confirmation and follow-up COMMUNITY NOTE Physical pharmacy open for advice and referral.
